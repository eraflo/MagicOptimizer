# Frequently asked questions

## Does the app work without a connection?

Yes — that is the whole point. After an initial download of the card data, everything works in
airplane mode: search, collection, deck building, optimization, scanning.

The connection is used for exactly three optional things: downloading data on first launch,
fetching card images for display, and refreshing prices.

## Is my data sent anywhere?

No. There is **no account, no telemetry, no server**. Your collection, decks, game log and
personal model stay on your device.

That is also why syncing between PC and phone happens through a file: there is no server to do it
for you.

## Why can't I import my MTG Arena collection?

Because it is not possible, and it is better to say so plainly.

Wizards of the Coast provides **no public API, no authentication and no Steam integration** for
Arena. The tools that manage it on PC read a local game log file, which is a fragile approach:
collection access was in fact removed from it in 2021, and what remains usable is uncertain.

On Android it is structurally impossible: one app cannot read another app's files.

The distinction between physical and digital collections still exists in the app, so this could be
added if the situation ever changes.

## Does it recognize cards in languages other than English?

Yes, with no configuration. Recognition works on the **artwork**, not the text, so the card's
language is irrelevant. French, Japanese, German and Italian cards all work equally well.

## Why is there no life counter?

A deliberate choice. The app is **never used during a game**: it is built for preparing beforehand
and recording afterwards.

Pulling out your phone at the table is frowned upon, and at competitive REL electronic devices are
banned outright. A live pick assistant would therefore be both unusable and inappropriate — which
is why draft pools are entered **after** the draft rather than during it.

## Is the project paid?

No, and it cannot be. The Wizards of the Coast Fan Content Policy, which is what permits this kind
of project, requires it to stay free and non-commercial.

## Can I reuse the code?

The code is MIT licensed, so yes. That license does **not** cover card data, names, text or
artwork, which remain the property of Wizards of the Coast.
